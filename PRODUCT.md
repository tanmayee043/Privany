# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

Chrome extension (Manifest V3 side panel) in `extension/` as the main way to use it; React + Vite + Tailwind (TypeScript) web app in `web/`; Node + Express + Puppeteer backend in `server/`. Policy reading is free and offline by default (rule-based); Claude is optional. Confirmed by the team.

## Users

Everyday web users, not privacy experts: someone about to sign up for a service, accept a cookie banner, or decide whether to keep using a site. They want a fast, plain-language answer to "can I trust this site with my data?" and will only dig into evidence if something looks wrong. Secondary audience: hackathon judges watching a 3-minute demo, who need to see the evidence and the method.

## Product Purpose

Privany checks whether a website's privacy promises hold up, and helps the user decide what to do. It reads the site's privacy policy, extracts specific promises with exact quotes, then visits the site in a controlled browser with and without Global Privacy Control (GPC) and records which tracking cookies are written. It sets each promise next to the observation and suggests next steps the policy actually documents. Success: a user understands, within a minute, where a site's words and behavior agree or diverge, and knows one concrete thing they can do.

## Positioning

Other tools summarize policies. Privany also tests behavior, using the dual-lens method from PrivAudit (CCS 2026, arXiv 2609.09697, UC Santa Cruz): policy disclosures vs. browser-observed cookie writes under privacy signals. PrivAudit produces aggregate statistics for researchers; Privany turns the method into an on-demand, single-site answer for one person.

## Operating Context

- A user opens a site's privacy policy (or any page of the site), clicks the Privany toolbar icon, and a side panel on the same page shows progress and then the report, in about 1–2 minutes. The web app offers the same from a pasted address.
- The report is read once, top-down, then specific findings are expanded for evidence.
- Demo context: shown live or replayed from saved reports on a projector during a 3-minute hackathon pitch.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Verdicts per promise: possible gap, consistent, not stated, not testable, inconclusive. Never "violation" or legal conclusions.
- Every policy quote shown has been verified word for word against the policy text; unsupported model answers are downgraded.
- Cookie purposes come from the Open Cookie Database; unknown cookies stay "unidentified".
- Observation scope: homepage plus one inner page, scrolled; no login; measured visits never click, while separate visits click the cookie banner (Reject All / Accept All) and a Play button or the page (click test); pop-ups are recorded and blocked before loading, downloads blocked; server location; cookies plus outside domains contacted (no fingerprinting/server-side).
- No numeric privacy score.
- Personal priorities are stored locally in the browser.
- Must run at no cost: the default policy reader is rule-based and offline; paid AI is opt-in only.
- Automatic mode (on by default, switchable): the extension observes every site's ad-tech requests and tracking cookies and reads its policy via the local server; it interrupts only for red-level sites. All data stays on the user's machine (localhost server). The full report still runs only on click.
- Undecided: site comparison, Reject-All consent test, opt-out request drafting, local AI model (planned add-ons).

## Brand Commitments

Name: Privany. Tagline is open to change (previous: "Understand the terms. Protect what matters."). Voice: calm, precise, plain-spoken; states uncertainty openly; never alarmist.

## Evidence on Hand

Real reports generated by the app against public sites (saved in `server/data/reports/`). No user testimonials, accuracy benchmarks, or partnerships exist yet; do not fabricate any.

## Product Principles

1. Evidence before opinion: every claim links to a quote or an observation.
2. Say what we don't know: unknowns, limits and alternative explanations are part of the answer.
3. Plain language first, detail on demand.
4. Action over summary: end with steps the user can actually take.
5. Privany itself collects nothing it doesn't need.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA: keyboard operable, visible focus, sufficient contrast, reduced-motion support; verdicts never rely on color alone.
